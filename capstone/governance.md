## 4. Review Cadence

### Routine Reviews

The plan is to review the process package once per quarter:

| Review Window | Target Date |
|---------------|-------------|
| Q2 2026 review | July 2026 |
| Q3 2026 review | October 2026 |
| Q4 2026 review | January 2027 |
| Q1 2027 review | April 2027 |

A quarterly cadence makes sense for a newer process. It gives Abbey a regular checkpoint to catch anything that isn't working before it becomes a bigger issue, without being so frequent that it's a burden.

### Trigger-Based Reviews

Some changes shouldn't wait for the annual review. A review should happen right away if:

- A new booking platform is adopted (e.g., switching from direct text to Rover or Wag)
- Care policies change (e.g., new rules on number of dogs, breed restrictions, or emergency vet authorization)
- An incident during a stay reveals a gap the current SOP didn't cover
- Client feedback keeps pointing to the same confusion or missing step
- The service expands into something new (dog walking, daycare, other animals, etc.)

If the triggering change affects how active bookings are handled, it's worth pausing new bookings until the SOP is updated.

## 5. Change Control

When something in the process package needs to change, here's the basic workflow:

1. **Identify:** Notice something that needs updating, whether from a scheduled review, an incident, or client feedback
2. **Evaluate:** Figure out the scope. Does it affect just the SOP, just the diagram, or both?
3. **Update:** Make the edits and increment the SOP version:
   - **Patch** (x.x.1): Typo fix, formatting cleanup, minor clarification with no real procedural change
   - **Minor** (x.1.0): Updated step, new decision rule, or clarified scope
   - **Major** (x.0.0): Big change to the overall process, roles, or structure
4. **Record:** Add a short note to the SOP governance header: version, date, what changed
5. **Regenerate:** If the BPMN diagram changed, export a new PDF before replacing the old one

## 6. Escalation

This is a one-person operation, so escalation is pretty straightforward.

| Situation | What to do |
|-----------|----------|
| A step in the SOP is unclear or missing a decision rule | Update the SOP, increment the version, note the change in the governance header |
| An incident during a stay reveals a gap | Handle the follow-up with the client first, then update the SOP before the next booking |
| A client disputes a care decision covered by the SOP | Resolve it directly with the client; update the SOP if the dispute points to real ambiguity |
| A process change affects what clients expect (e.g., new meet and greet policy) | Let active clients know before the change takes effect; update the SOP to match |
