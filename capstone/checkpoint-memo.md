## 4. If I handed your current draft to the intended user today, what would confuse them first?

The current draft assumes the reader already knows what they are looking at. If Abbey handed the SOP to another dog sitter who had never seen a formal process document, the first thing that would trip them up is the lack of orientation — there is no README or knowledge base article that explains what the SOP is, how to use it, or what to expect from the process before they start reading. The content is accurate, but there is no on-ramp.

The same problem exists with the BPMN diagram. The gateway symbols and swimlane layout are standard notation, but they are not intuitive to someone who has never seen a process diagram before.

This is something I have seen with my own 3D printer SOP. If you already know how 3D printing works, the SOP makes immediate sense. If you have never done it, the SOP alone is not enough — you need a short tutorial or introductory article to tell you what you are about to do before the step-by-step instructions become useful. A knowledge base article sitting in front of the SOP would solve that problem for Abbey's process as well.
